description: What reports, maps and sample records to request before exploring a mineral area in Kazakhstan. A practical checklist and a worked example.
---

Before deciding whether to explore a mineral area, gather the source materials and check that they cover the same area and support the claims being made. Start with reports, maps with legends, sample records, and the documents behind any resource or reserve estimates. Check rights and restrictions affecting the area separately. A polished presentation, one high-grade sample, or an old reserve figure is not enough to make a decision.

This review is often called geological due diligence: checking source data and risks. What follows is a starting point for a discussion with a geologist, not a full project assessment, valuation, or KAZRC/JORC report.

## First define the decision you need to make

“Is there gold here?” is too broad a question. Define the next step instead: choosing a region to study, comparing two proposals, checking an old report, or preparing a fieldwork programme. That choice determines which materials you need now and which uncertainties you can accept.

Write down the region, the metal of interest, the area's outline or landmarks, and your question. Do not call an area a “deposit with reserves” if the only evidence so far is a mention of a mineral occurrence. The basic terms are explained in [what is geology](/en/insights/what-is-geology).

## What to request before discussing conclusions

| Material                              | What to establish                                                                       | Question left unanswered without it                                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Report and list of appendices         | Title, author, year of work, archive reference number, and contents of the set received | What work does the report actually describe, and what is missing from the files supplied? |
| Map and area plan                     | Scale, legend, coordinate system, coverage, and how the map was positioned              | Do the data relate to the area of interest?                                               |
| Sampling records                      | Where, when, and how samples were taken; how sample numbers connect to results          | What exactly do the stated grades describe?                                               |
| Laboratory analysis records           | Method, units, quality control, and links to the original samples                       | Can the origin and comparability of the figures be checked?                               |
| Resource or reserve estimate document | Author, date, reporting standard, category, and underlying assumptions                  | What does the number mean, and for which decision may it be used?                         |
| Rights and restrictions information   | Source, date, area boundary, and document details                                       | Does the area's legal status permit the intended activity?                                |

The last row calls for a separate legal review. A geological description cannot replace it. This table is not an exhaustive document list: the scope of a review depends on the project's stage and the question being asked.

## Where to begin looking for reports in Kazakhstan

The [Kazakhstan Ministry of Industry and Construction describes the Unified Subsoil Use Platform (EPN) register](https://www.gov.kz/memleket/entities/mps/press/news/details/945522?lang=ru): the platform contains information on geological reports and initial volumes with abstracts, contents, lists of graphical appendices, and conclusions. Open the [geological reports register](https://minerals.e-qazyna.kz/ru/guest/reestr/geological-reports) and search for a known report title or area.

An abstract is only the start of a search. Compare the list of appendices with what is actually available: access to the first volume does not mean you have the maps, logs, or all assay results. Ask the official service about missing materials and access procedures. We discuss analysis of these materials, not the sale of government archive reports.

## Three checks that help reveal gaps

**Is it the same area?** Compare the outline of the area of interest with the report's coverage and the sample locations. If names, coordinate systems, or positioning differ, record the discrepancy and find out why. Do not align an old scan with a modern map by eye. See the [geological map guide](/en/insights/geological-map-kazakhstan) for choosing a scale and source.

**Can the assay results be traced?** [Table 1 of the JORC Code 2012](https://www.jorc.org/docs/JORC_code_2012.pdf) covers sampling methods and representativeness, sample preparation, laboratory data quality, control samples, and verification of results. These are useful questions when first reviewing a set of materials. JORC is used here as a reference for questions about data; completing this checklist does not make the material a JORC report or mean that the code is mandatory for every project in Kazakhstan.

**Has a number changed meaning?** A highest-grade sample, average grade, prospective resources, and reserves answer different questions. First find the source document and the label attached to the figure. Reporting systems cannot be made equivalent simply by changing a label; see the [guide to GKZ, KAZRC and JORC](/en/insights/reserve-classification-gkz-kazrc-jorc-gbt17766).

## Example: a presentation says “gold up to 20 g/t”

This is a **hypothetical teaching example, not data from a QAZNEDR area**. That single line cannot establish an average grade or a quantity of metal. The first question is where the figure came from: an individual sample, a drill-hole interval, a group of assays, or a calculation for a defined volume?

For an initial review, request the relevant page of the source report, the sample locations and descriptions, the linked assay results, and the method used to derive the stated figure. If these materials are unavailable, record in your working table: “The source and representativeness of this figure have not yet been checked.” It is also too early to conclude that “the area is poor”: a lack of confirmation and a negative result from a check are different situations.

## What the initial review should produce

A useful result is a list of materials checked and questions still open. For each conclusion, state the source, limitation, and next step. For example: “The map covers the relevant area, but the sampling points have not been positioned; compare coordinates first.” This makes the scope of further work easier to discuss.

Useful columns for a working table are **question — source — what was checked — what remains unknown — next step**. Do not replace an unknown with zero or with a favourable estimate. At this stage, you may find that the first task is to locate an appendix to an existing report, rather than commission new fieldwork.

A geological review alone is insufficient for an investment decision: legal, technical, environmental, and economic questions require separate work by the relevant specialists. The [separate guide](/en/insights/solid-minerals-exploration-licence-kazakhstan) explains the licensing process.

## How to discuss materials with QAZNEDR

Start with a short message: **“Hello! I am contacting you through qaznedr.kz. I need a review of geological materials for an area in [region].”** Then explain the task and which documents you already have; we will agree separately on how to share the materials. The scope of work depends on the source materials available. We cannot promise a result on reserves, licensing, or investment attractiveness in advance.

You can contact the team through [WhatsApp or WeChat](/en/contact). Exact coordinates and additional materials for areas presented by the team are disclosed after a meeting and a signed NDA.
