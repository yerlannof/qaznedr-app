Most of Kazakhstan's recorded gold reserves are in the east of the country and in the Akmola and Karaganda regions. According to the National Report on the Implementation of the Extractive Industries Transparency Initiative (EITI) for 2022, published on the website of the Ministry of Industry and Construction in October 2025, East Kazakhstan region holds 36% of the gold reserves, Akmola 21% and Karaganda 11%. The report does not give the date of the count and does not say whether the regions are counted within their old or new boundaries. According to the U.S. Geological Survey (USGS) Minerals Yearbook for 2023, the leading gold producers are JSC Altyntau-Kokshetau in Akmola region and Polymetal in East Kazakhstan and Kostanay regions; AK Altynalmas operates in Karaganda and Zhambyl regions.

Below are a table by region, how a "gold map" differs from other maps, useful scales and how to check an area. No map on its own tells you that an area is free or that gold can be mined there.

## How much gold is in the ground: figures disagree

Overall estimates in public sources do not match. At a forum in Astana, the Vice Minister of Industry and Construction put gold reserves at 2.3 thousand tonnes (Forbes.kz, 2 April 2026). USGS, citing the World Gold Council, gives about 1,000 tonnes at the end of 2022. Neither source names a reserve category under the Kazakhstan State Reserves Committee (GKZ) classification or another estimation standard, so the figures cannot be compared directly.

According to the EITI report, in 2024 17 deposits of various minerals were placed on the state register; for gold they hold 107.5 t of reserves and 337.04 t of resources. Reserves and resources are different categories, and prognostic resources are not reserves. More in the [guide to reserve classification](/en/insights/reserve-classification-gkz-kazrc-jorc-gbt17766).

## Gold by region

The gold districts in the table are named after the work of V. A. Narseev and co-authors (1970). It identifies 13 gold ore districts in Kazakhstan, where more than 1,000 mineralisation points, ore occurrences and gold deposits were known. Deposits and their operators are given according to the USGS Minerals Yearbooks for 2022 and 2023 and official publications; the operator may have changed.

All deposits in the table are **other parties' objects**: other companies mine or hold them. QAZNEDR does not own them and does not offer them.

In 2022 Abai region was split off from East Kazakhstan, Ulytau from Karaganda and Zhetysu from Almaty (USGS). These pairs are therefore combined in one row. Matching the historical districts to today's regions is approximate: a district may extend into a neighbouring region.

How to read the table:

- The 1970 districts are historical names. They help you search for reports but do not match the boundaries of today's regions or licences.
- The lode deposit types follow USGS (2022) and the 1970 article. In 1970 the authors wrote that Kazakhstan supplied only lode gold and that placers played a minor role in production.
- A region with no deposit listed near you does not mean there is no gold there. The table lists only objects described in public sources.

### What primary material exists

Several kinds of material exist for all regions. By 1970, forecast metallogenic maps for gold at 1:500,000 and 1:200,000 had been compiled for Kazakhstan (Narseev et al., 1970). The former scale of state mapping, developed in Soviet times, is 1:200,000. Geological reports on completed work are collected in the [EPN register](https://minerals.e-qazyna.kz/ru/guest/reestr/geological-reports). Some districts have research papers: for example, the ancient placers of West Kalba are analysed in a 2020 paper from Tomsk Polytechnic University. Companies publish their own annual reports on operating mines.

Geological information in electronic form is provided through the Unified Subsoil Use Platform, EPN (Article 75 of the Subsoil Code). QAZNEDR does not sell state archive reports and is not a state body.

## How a "gold map" differs from other maps

A search for "gold map of Kazakhstan" can lead to four different materials:

| Material                                  | What it shows                                                             | What it does not prove                                                         |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Map of gold, deposits and ore occurrences | Where deposits, ore occurrences, anomalies and prognostic areas are known | That an object is free, or that its reserves are confirmed under current rules |
| Geological map                            | Rocks, their age, faults, intrusions — the geological context             | Commercial gold at a specific point                                            |
| EPN licence map                           | Information on granted licences and contracts as of the viewing date      | The presence of gold                                                           |
| PUGFN                                     | How the state makes a territory available                                 | Any specific party's rights or the area's geology                              |

Open access to information on granted subsoil use rights is provided on the Unified Subsoil Use Platform (Article 77). The state subsoil fund management programme (PUGFN) is published in open access on the competent authority's website and on the same platform (Article 70).

How to choose and check a geological map is covered in the [guide to geological maps of Kazakhstan](/en/insights/geological-map-kazakhstan), and how to read its legend and cross-section in [how to read a geological map](/en/insights/how-to-read-geological-map). What PUGFN is and how an area is checked against it is in the [PUGFN guide](/en/insights/pugfn-subsoil-fund-program-kazakhstan). For the east of the country there is a separate [East Kazakhstan gold map guide](/en/insights/east-kazakhstan-gold-map-guide), and for what a satellite image can show, the [satellite map guide](/en/insights/satellite-gold-map-guide).

## Which map sheets and scales to use for gold

**1:200,000** — 1 cm on the map equals 2 km on the ground. This is the former mapping scale developed in Soviet times (Ministry of Industry and Construction, January 2026). The forecast metallogenic maps for gold were also compiled at this scale. A 1:200,000 sheet helps you see which gold district, belt or ore field your area falls into and which objects are known nearby.

**1:50,000** — 1 cm equals 500 m. The Committee of Geology calls such mapping the basic level of detailed study: it defines the composition, age and extent of rocks and the position of faults and igneous bodies more precisely, and allows a move from regional generalisations to conclusions tied to specific areas (January 2026). According to the ministry, 20 projects for 1:50,000 surveying have been developed over 100 thousand km², followed by annual coverage of 30 thousand km² of the most promising areas. Results of state geological study of the subsoil are published in open access on EPN within two months of the signing of the certificate of completed work (Article 74).

In practice: find the nomenclature number of the 1:200,000 sheet for your area, then check whether 1:50,000 material exists for it. Enlarging a 1:200,000 scan to 1:50,000 does not add field observations. For each sheet, record the year, author and legend.

## Why a point on a map does not mean a free area

The subsoil belongs to the people of Kazakhstan; the state exercises ownership on their behalf and grants the use of the subsoil under the Code (Article 11). A subsoil use right arises only from a licence or a contract (Article 20). A deposit symbol on any map gives neither.

The well-known deposits in the table are, as a rule, already in use. An old working, a historical ore occurrence or a prognostic area may lie inside someone else's area, in a restricted zone or under the auction procedure. Therefore:

1. Write down the coordinates or the block number of the area.
2. Check it on the [EPN interactive map](https://minerals-map.e-qazyna.kz/ru/start) as of the decision date for existing licences and contracts.
3. Compare the territory's category with the current PUGFN.
4. Keep the check date and a screenshot — the status changes.

A screenshot of someone else's map without a date does not confirm the current status. Reserves, even when shown on a map, need a date and an estimation standard — GKZ, KAZRC or a historical estimate.

## Lode and placer gold: who can do what

**Lode (bedrock) gold** sits in hard rock — in quartz veins, stockworks and mineralised zones. It is searched for and evaluated under a solid minerals exploration licence, which gives the right to search for deposits and assess their resources and reserves (Article 185). Mining requires a solid minerals mining licence (Article 202).

**Placer gold** is particles in loose sediments of rivers, gullies and slopes. Dredging and hydraulic mining of placers counts as solid minerals mining (Article 202) and also requires a mining licence.

**Artisanal mining** is a separate regime only for placer and technogenic deposits (dumps and waste from mining and metallurgy), worked by hand or with low-power equipment (Article 263). A lode deposit cannot be worked under an artisanal mining licence. Only a citizen of Kazakhstan can hold the licence, one licence per person (Article 263). Areas for artisanal mining are set by the regional akimat; licences are not issued within settlements or within 1,000 metres of them (Article 264). An artisanal miner may extract no more than 50 kg of placer gold per calendar year (Article 270).

More: [artisanal mining licence](/en/insights/artisanal-mining-licence-kazakhstan), [placer gold](/en/insights/placer-gold-kazakhstan) and [choosing a gold licence](/en/insights/gold-licence-types-kazakhstan).

## Where to start

Write down the region, the nearest settlement or coordinates, the type of gold you expect — lode or placer — and the materials you have: map sheets with scale and year, reports, sample results. General context on the metal is in the [overview of gold in Kazakhstan](/en/minerals/gold). The [areas page](/en/leads) publishes descriptions of areas selected by our geologists; the status of each area is checked as of the date shown, and publishing a description does not mean that QAZNEDR holds the right to the area. [Describe your task](/en/contact?service=geology) and our geologists will help review the materials and decide on the next verification step. We do not promise a discovery or a licensing outcome in advance.
