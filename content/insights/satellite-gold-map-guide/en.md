---
title: 'Can you find gold in Kazakhstan on a satellite map?'
description: 'What satellite images can show a geologist, how surface signs of altered rock are identified, and why other evidence is needed to confirm gold.'
---

The short answer: a satellite image can help you notice surface features and decide what to check next. The image itself does not confirm gold, its grade or its reserves. From space, sensors measure reflected light, heat or other surface properties. They do not directly measure the composition of rock at depth. A satellite map is therefore one source for a geological hypothesis, not a ready answer about a deposit.

This distinction matters if you are searching for a “gold deposit on a satellite map”. An attractive colour map or an unusual area in an image does not mean that gold is present, that it can be mined or that anyone holds rights to the area.

## What a satellite can actually show

Different sensors record surface properties: reflected light in different bands, heat or radar texture. Where rock is exposed and imaging conditions are suitable, spectral features can help distinguish some surface minerals and zones of altered rock.

The USGS describes using ASTER data to map mineral groups, vegetation and types of hydrothermal alteration. This provides useful geological context: some systems of altered rock may be associated with mineralisation and help set a direction for further checks. But a satellite map shows interpreted surface features, not a chemical assay for gold or a measurement of its quantity.

Minerals associated with alteration occur in different settings. Even a well-defined alteration zone is not the same as a gold deposit. It needs to be compared with the geological setting and independent observations.

## Why a satellite map cannot answer whether there is gold

A satellite observes the upper surface. An ore body may lie beneath soil, loose deposits or vegetation, and the mineral of interest may have no surface feature distinguishable by the chosen sensor. ESA notes that optical instruments cannot see through vegetation. Maps developed for exposed, arid areas may therefore be less useful in vegetated places.

Season, lighting, moisture, clouds, resolution and processing all affect an image. A colour on a thematic map usually represents the result of a chosen classification. Without the source, date, scale and processing description, its meaning is unclear.

A satellite also cannot determine the legal status of land. Whether an area is available for particular work and which rights are recorded in official systems must be checked separately against official information as of the decision date. An image does not confirm ownership, a licence, reserves or future returns.

## How the main sources differ

| Source          | What it can help establish                                                                       | What it cannot confirm on its own                                                                       |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Satellite image | Visible structures and surface spectral features; a possible direction for further checks        | Gold in rock, its grade, reserves or land rights                                                        |
| Geological map  | Known rocks, contacts, structures and geological context, taking its scale and date into account | The exact structure below the surface or confirmed reserves at a specific point                         |
| Geochemistry    | Element concentrations in samples taken at specific places and intervals                         | That one sample's result applies to an entire area without justification                                |
| Drilling        | The sequence of rocks at depth and material for description and sampling                         | Reserves without a sufficient network of data, analysis and an assessment under the applicable standard |

These sources answer different questions and can complement one another. The right sequence of checks depends on the area, available data and the specific geological hypothesis. For background on these methods, see [USGS on remote sensing of minerals](https://www.usgs.gov/science/remote-sensing-minerals) and [ESA on satellite mapping of geology and mineral resources](https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Sentinels_helping_to_map_minerals).

## How to use an image thoughtfully

Start by checking the source: the image name and date, sensor, resolution, spectral bands, processing method and legend. Note vegetation and loose surface deposits. Compare the image's coverage, scale and legend with a geological map. Do not draw precise boundaries of a possible body from an overview image.

Describe an observation carefully: “The image highlights a zone interpreted as alteration of surface rocks; it needs to be checked against a geological map and ground data.” This keeps the observation separate from a conclusion that has not yet been tested.

Further work may require geological records, a ground survey, geochemical samples and justified drilling with documented samples and assays. Reserves require sufficient data and an assessment stating its date, category and applicable standard, such as GKZ or KAZRC.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an image show native gold itself?

An ordinary satellite map does not show gold grains or gold grade. It provides indirect information about surface minerals and forms; samples and assays are needed to check whether the metal is present.

### Can a satellite help find a promising area?

Remote sensing data can help compare areas and frame a hypothesis. Their usefulness depends on the sensor, exposed rock, vegetation, scale and interpretation. They provide a basis for choosing further checks, not a conclusion that an area is promising.

### Can an image show whether an area is available?

No. Geological and satellite data do not replace checking rights and restrictions in official sources as of the relevant date. That check is separate.
