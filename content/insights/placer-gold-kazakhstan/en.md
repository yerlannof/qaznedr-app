Placer gold is gold particles in loose sediments: river sands and gravels, slopes, gullies and ancient valleys. It is recovered by washing, and it is searched for and evaluated differently from lode gold. But a placer marked on a map at some point does not mean anyone may work there: the right to a specific area arises only from a licence or a contract.

## Where the gold in a placer comes from

A placer forms when its bedrock source — gold-bearing quartz veins or ore zones — breaks down. Water and slope processes move the released gold particles. Gold is heavier than the surrounding sand and pebbles, so it settles where the flow loses energy: at the base of the loose sediments on bedrock, on riffles, in hollows of the channel floor and at the mouths of gullies.

Hence the main search rule: a placer is tied to its source and to the landscape in which it formed. A study of the West Kalba gold district names the spatial link between placers and their bedrock sources as the main pattern of their distribution.

## Placer types

The reference book “Placer Gold Deposits of Kazakhstan” (Almaty, 1999) distinguishes several geological and industrial placer types in the country:

| Type               | Where it occurs                                  | What to keep in mind                                               |
| ------------------ | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Alluvial           | River channels, floodplains and terraces         | Terraces can lie above the present river; channels shift over time |
| Alluvial-colluvial | Gullies and hollows on slopes                    | The source is often further up the slope                           |
| Alluvial-proluvial | Alluvial fans at the foot of mountains           | Large volumes of sand, often fine gold                             |
| Eluvial-colluvial  | Surface and slopes above the bedrock source      | The placer lies close to where the source broke down               |
| Coastal, karst     | Shores of ancient lakes and seas, karst cavities | Less common, need a dedicated assessment                           |
| Technogenic        | Dumps of old workings and tailings               | Check that the dumps are not part of someone else's area           |

A separate group is ancient (Paleogene and Neogene) placers in buried valleys and intermontane depressions. In West Kalba they formed from the Oligocene to the Pliocene; the depressions hold large-volume placers dominated by fine and very fine gold. The authors of a 2020 study consider them potentially more productive than Quaternary placers, but note that they have still not been comprehensively assessed.

## Where placers are historically known in Kazakhstan

According to the 1999 reference book, the core of the country’s placer gold resource base is the alluvial placers of **Kalba, the Southern Altai, Dzungaria, Karatau and the Zailiysky (Trans-Ili) Alatau**. Within today’s borders this means above all the east of the country (East Kazakhstan Region and Abai Region), as well as mountain districts of the south and south-east.

How to use this list:

- These are names of natural districts, not addresses of free areas. Some known placers have been mined out, and rights may already be held over others.
- The reference book describes the situation as of 1999. Recent work and the current status of an area are checked separately.
- A district name on a map says nothing about the gold content of a particular stream. You need samples, their location and the year of the work.

For the east there are separate guides: the [East Kazakhstan gold map](/en/insights/east-kazakhstan-gold-map-guide) and the [overview of gold in Kazakhstan](/en/minerals/gold). Lode districts and well-known deposits for every region are collected in the [gold map of Kazakhstan](/en/insights/gold-map-kazakhstan) guide.

## How geologists search for placer gold

1. **Geological map and landscape.** Look for bedrock sources — zones with lode gold occurrences — and for valleys, gullies, terraces and depressions downslope of them. Reading the legend and cross-section is covered in the [geological map guide](/en/insights/how-to-read-geological-map).
2. **Archive data.** Old reports show where samples were already washed, where there were workings and what came of them. The report register is on the Unified Subsoil Use Platform (EPN); for each source record the year, scale and location.
3. **Satellite imagery.** It helps to see ancient valleys, terraces and traces of old workings. This is how the West Kalba study refined the outlines of ancient valleys and depressions. The limits of imagery are explained in the [satellite map guide](/en/insights/satellite-gold-map-guide): an image does not show gold by itself.
4. **Heavy-mineral (panned concentrate) sampling.** Samples of loose sediment are washed down to the heavy fraction, the concentrate, and gold grains are counted. It is a quick way to tell whether a valley carries gold and which way to move upstream. The number of grains in a concentrate is not the gold grade of the sands.
5. **Pits, trenches and drilling.** Evaluating a placer requires samples through the full thickness of the sands down to bedrock, with locations and volumes recorded. Only such data support statements about grade in grams per cubic metre and about reserves.

## How to start legally

Subsoil belongs to the people of Kazakhstan; the state exercises ownership on their behalf and grants subsoil for use under the Subsoil Code (Article 11). A subsoil use right arises from a licence or a contract (Article 20). Even manual artisanal mining is carried out under a licence (Article 263).

The regime depends on the scale:

- **Artisanal mining** is the extraction of precious metals from placer and technogenic deposits by hand or with low-power equipment (Article 263). Only a citizen of Kazakhstan may hold the licence, one person may hold one licence, and it cannot be transferred. Areas for artisanal mining are designated by the regional akimat; licences are not granted within settlements or within 1,000 metres of them, or on a number of protected lands (Article 264). The application steps are in the [artisanal mining licence guide](/en/insights/artisanal-mining-licence-kazakhstan).
- **Exploration and industrial mining.** Dredging and hydraulic mining of placers count as solid minerals mining (Article 202) and require a mining licence. If the placer still has to be studied, start with an exploration licence. The differences are set out in the [comparison of gold licences](/en/insights/gold-licence-types-kazakhstan).

Before applying, check the status of the area on the EPN map as of the decision date. A known placer district, an old working or a point on a map does not mean the area is free.

## Equipment, water and land restoration

An artisanal licence limits the scale of work (Article 270): drilling and earthworks no deeper than three metres below the lowest point of the area’s surface; no more than 50 kg of placer gold per calendar year; no chemical reagents or explosives; no removal of soil or rock mass beyond the area.

The Code requires artisanal miners not to destroy the natural relief of riverbanks, riverbeds and lake beds, and to restore disturbed land (Article 271). On water bodies and water fund land, artisanal mining is done by hand only. Mechanised work requires an artisanal mining plan with a positive state environmental review (Article 273). Closure of artisanal operations — restoring the disturbed land — must be completed within six months after the licence ends and is complete once a commission signs the closure act (Article 273-1); performance of this obligation is secured in advance by a pledge of a bank deposit or a bank guarantee (Article 274).

Budget for this work from the start, not after the season ends.

## Where to start

Write down the district, the nearest settlement or coordinates, the placer type you expect and the materials you have: maps, reports, sample results. The [areas page](/en/leads) publishes descriptions of areas selected by our geologists; the status of each area is checked as of the date shown, and publishing a description does not mean that QAZNEDR holds the right to the area. [Describe your task](/en/contact?service=geology) and our geologists will help review the materials and decide on the next verification step. We do not promise a discovery or a licensing outcome in advance.
